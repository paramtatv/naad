The two promises

Nothing lost. Nothing hidden.

01

Identical. Not “transparent”.

The samples you encoded are the samples you get back — the same integers, every one. We will never tell you a lossless format sounds warmer; that claim is how you spot someone who has not done the arithmetic. We hand you the conformance suite instead.

02

Six times real time, with no C.

1,814 instructions per channel-sample on a 1 GHz core, through a decoder with zero lines of compiled-language code beneath it. Most codecs buy their speed with a C runtime and a dependency tree. Naad has neither.

03

Level with FLAC, on FLAC's ground.

All 64 files of the IETF test corpus — a set built to break decoders — cleared, at the same size flac -8 produces. Twenty years of tuning, met by Levinson–Durbin, apodisation and a full Rice partition search written in Sanskrit.

All 64 subframe type codes

Not a selection — 39 legal codes and 25 reserved ones, each classified or refused. Reserved codes are refused rather than masked: a decoder that masks three bits and trusts them accepts a predictor order that was never written.

All four frame-header code spaces

16 blocksize codes, 16 sample-rate codes, 16 channel assignments, 8 bit depths — and both escape forms displaced by a seven-octet frame number, because the escaped field sits after the number, not beside the code that triggers it.

Both CRCs anchored externally

CRC-8 gives 0xF4 and CRC-16/UMTS gives 0xFEE8 over "123456789" — published catalogue values. A CRC checked only against another implementation of the same polynomial agrees on every wrong polynomial too.

Fourteen deliberate defects, each caught

The suite runs against a sabotaged decoder — wasted bits ignored, Rice parameter width hardcoded, the side channel not widened — and every mutation is caught at the exact case built to catch it.

Two independent engines must agree

Every result is produced twice: once by an interpreter executing the Sanskrit source, once by the compiled RISC-V image. A green requires both. One engine agreeing with itself is not evidence.

A foreign reference, on purpose

The oracle every result is checked against is written in another language entirely. A reference written in Sanskrit and built by the same compiler would be a twin — and a twin cannot be a witness.

The technology

Sassembly — a systems language whose every keyword is Sanskrit.

Not a transliteration layer. Not a DSL over C. A complete instruction set architecture and language, royalty-free, compiling to 64-bit RISC-V — and written in itself. Built at Paramtatva, a frontier computing lab.

12MB

The entire toolchain

Compiler, assembler, linker, interpreter. No LLVM, no GCC, no package manager, nothing fetched at build time. Small enough to read, to keep, to carry somewhere with no network.

0

Floats in the decode path

Fitting the predictor is float work and stays in the encoder. Quantising those coefficients to integers plus a shift is the boundary — past it every operation is exact, and nothing can round differently.

2

Engines that must agree

An interpreter executing the source, and the compiled RISC-V image. Both must produce the same answer before a result counts. One engine agreeing with itself is not evidence, and we do not accept it as such.

The compiler compiles itself — byte for byte.

Feed Sassembly's source to Sassembly and it produces a compiler image. Feed the source to that image and it produces another — byte for byte identical. That is a reached fixpoint, and very few toolchains on Earth can demonstrate one. There is no privileged binary anywhere in the chain, nothing to take on faith, no step where you trust a vendor instead of reading source. The stack is not merely open. It is auditable to the bottom — and Naad sits on top of it.

The vocabulary turns out to be load-bearing. The decoder is called निःशेषम् — niḥśeṣam, “with no remainder” — not a poetic name for lossless decoding but a precise definition of it. नाद — nāda — is sound considered as vibration rather than as signal.

Open problems · where we are still behind

The problems we have not solved yet.

A codec that will not tell you where it is weak is one you cannot plan around. These are the places Naad is behind the state of the art, what the gap measures, and what we already know about closing it.

Active · throughput

Raw decode speed

Mature C decoders run well ahead of six times real time. Naad is fast enough to play anything on anything — not yet fast enough to be the obvious choice for a battery-powered device decoding for ten hours.

Profiled to the instruction. CRC-16 alone is 37% of decode time — it re-traverses every bit of the frame to gather octets. An octet-wise path measures at 1.60× on the whole decoder before any other work.
Active · compression

Beating FLAC, not matching it

Level is the entry ticket, not the destination. Real gains now lie in longer-context prediction and adaptive block switching — neither of which the FLAC bitstream format was designed to carry.

The encoder's size figure is an exact bit count of the coded stream. The join — that a stream of exactly that size decodes back — is not yet closed.
In build · format

Container, seeking, metadata

The frame decoder is complete and verified on two engines. Walking a file of frames, seeking to a timestamp, reading tags and cover art, and recovering from a damaged frame are the layer above it.

Frame layer complete. Stream layer in build.
In build · channels

Beyond two channels

Mono and every stereo pairing are done and verified. Surround and ambisonic material parses its header today and is then refused rather than guessed at.

An unimplemented path that answers is not the same as one that quietly returns something plausible.
Research · perceptual

Lossy, without the usual bargain

Every lossy codec in wide use discards what a psychoacoustic model from the 1990s decided you could not hear. There is a great deal of published work since, and almost none of it has reached a shipping format.

Nothing here ships yet. We will not describe it as available until it is measured.
Research · the long horizon

Sound as structure, not as samples

A recording is a list of amplitudes. Music is not. The Indian tradition spent centuries building a vocabulary for pitch, interval and ornament that is structural rather than statistical — and a codec that understood structure would not be competing on the same axis at all.

This is the thesis the project is actually chasing. It is research, and this page marks it as research.
